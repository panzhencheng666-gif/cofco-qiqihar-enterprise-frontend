# Market intelligence map assets

`terrain/{z}/{x}/{y}.webp` is a local XYZ pyramid (zoom 0–4, 256 px, WebP quality 83) generated from Natural Earth's **Natural Earth I with Shaded Relief and Water**, 1:50m raster, version 3.2.0 (`NE1_50M_SR_W.tif`). The original GeoTIFF is not committed. Source: https://www.naturalearthdata.com/downloads/50m-natural-earth-1/50m-natural-earth-i-with-shaded-relief-and-water/

`natural-earth-ocean-50m.geojson` is derived from the 1:50m ocean polygon (`ne_50m_ocean`), clipped into 45-degree cells to avoid antimeridian wrapping in MapLibre. Source: https://www.naturalearthdata.com/downloads/50m-physical-vectors/

Natural Earth explicitly places its raster and vector data in the public domain and permits commercial use, modification, and redistribution: https://www.naturalearthdata.com/about/terms-of-use/

`populated-places-10m.geojson` is a compact extract of Natural Earth 1:10m populated places v5.1.2 (`SCALERANK <= 4`, 1,128 points). It preserves the source coordinates, priority rank, capital flag, and the provided Chinese place name where available. Source: https://www.naturalearthdata.com/downloads/10m-cultural-vectors/10m-populated-places/

`shipping-lanes-2012.geojson` is the unchanged Global Shipping Lanes v1 dataset by P. Benden, derived from the CIA 2012 world oceans map. It is a **historical route reference**, not a live AIS feed or current navigational advice. The accompanying `SHIPPING-LANES-LICENSE.txt` is the upstream license file, which describes CC BY 4.0 with an author exception for Statista. The upstream README inconsistently calls the license CC BY-SA; resolve that discrepancy with the author before production distribution. Source and citation: https://github.com/newzealandpaul/Shipping-Lanes and https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.6361763.

At all zoom levels, the map displays VersaTiles' hosted satellite layer (`https://tiles.versatiles.org/tiles/satellite/{z}/{x}/{y}`), with the local Natural Earth pyramid underneath as a fallback. The service returns 512 px tiles; MapLibre treats each as a 256 px display tile to retain detail on high-density screens. A 1:50m ocean polygon darkens the sea at global zoom. The satellite layer combines Sentinel-2 Global Mosaic (CC BY 4.0) and NASA Blue Marble (public domain). The hosted service is documented for prototyping and small projects; an enterprise deployment should self-host a geographically bounded extract or agree on service capacity. Satellite mosaic imagery is historical and must not be represented as a live observation. Sources and attribution: https://docs.versatiles.org/basics/tilesets.html and https://versatiles.org/sources/.

The source raster is color graded in RGB before tiling: `vegetation = clamp((2G - R - B - 12) / 50, 0, 1)` and `gradedRGB = originalRGB × (0.64 - 0.34 × vegetation) × (1 - vegetation × [0.19, 0.05, 0.26])`. An ocean mask rasterized from the 1:50m Natural Earth ocean polygon selects a separate bathymetry preserving grade: `oceanRGB = clamp([9, 24, 34] + (originalRGB - [119, 169, 205]) × [0.28, 0.45, 0.50], [2, 2, 2], [22, 44, 58])`. These are visual treatments, not current satellite images or vegetation observations. Tiles were generated with GDAL `gdal2tiles.py -p mercator --xyz -z 0-4 --tiledriver=WEBP --webp-quality=88 --processes=4 --webviewer=none -r average graded.tif terrain`. The ocean GeoJSON was produced by `ogr2ogr -clipsrc` over a 45-degree longitude/latitude grid and concatenating the 32 resulting features.

Rebuild the grade with `gdal_rasterize -burn 1 -te -180 -90 180 90 -ts 10800 5400 -ot Byte -of GTiff ne_50m_ocean.shp ocean-mask.tif`, then `python3 scripts/market-intelligence/grade-terrain.py NE1_50M_SR_W.tif ocean-mask.tif graded.tif`. The original source downloads and intermediate rasters are intentionally outside the frontend repository.

Glyphs, country boundary vectors, and city/town labels at zoom 7+ still load from OpenFreeMap at runtime; OpenFreeMap, OpenMapTiles, and OpenStreetMap are credited in the UI as specified by the project: https://github.com/hyperknot/openfreemap#attribution. An enterprise deployment needs an approved or self-hosted vector tile service and an availability plan. The local terrain pyramid is a geographic background, not a live imagery feed or a market observation. It does not claim current satellite acquisition dates.
